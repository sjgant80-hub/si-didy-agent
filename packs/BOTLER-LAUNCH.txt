"I would not trust that secretary."

That sentence is the most accurate critique of every AI assistant on the market right now.

The standard pattern: you give the assistant access to your memory, your calendar, your email. They process it on their servers. They retain logs "for service improvement." They train future models on your data unless you remember to opt out. They could be subpoenaed. They could be breached. They could pivot. They could be sold. They could lose your CEO.

The trust problem cannot be solved by policy. Every "we don't sell your data" promise relies on a future executive at the company keeping that promise. Every "we encrypt at rest" promise is undone by anyone with database access. Every "we comply with GDPR" is at most a defence against fines, not a defence against access.

The trust problem can be solved by architecture.

Not by adding more rules. By removing the third party from the data path.

That's what botler is. The AI runs in your browser. The memory lives in your IndexedDB. The OAuth tokens are stored on your device. There is no server because there is no need for one.

You don't have to trust me · because nothing reaches me to be trusted with.

If you trust your hard drive with your documents, you can trust botler with your memory.

Same storage. Same access. Same security.

I built this because Alex needed it · and because every "AI productivity tool" that asked for my Gmail was a permission I couldn't justify.

POST 3 · FOR BUILDERS (architecture · day 8)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
audience: developers · privacy-tech builders · "I want to build this for X"
hook: the technical receipt of how a single-file sovereign agent works
length: ~2,600 chars

▼ COPY ▼
The architecture of botler · for builders who want to ship sovereign agents.

Single HTML file. ~1,900 lines of vanilla JS. No framework. No build step. No backend.

Five core decisions:

1 · WebLLM via ESM dynamic import.
The model (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct-q4f16_1-MLC) loads on first use through `import('https://esm.run/@mlc-ai/web-llm')`. ~2GB downloads once · cached in OPFS · runs offline forever after. No GPU required — WebGPU fallback handles older devices. T0 (grep-only) works without it for users on slow connections.

2 · OAuth 2.0 PKCE flow, client-side.
S256 challenge generated via crypto.subtle.digest. Verifier in sessionStorage (clears on tab close). Authorization code exchanged for token directly with Google's token endpoint from the browser. Token persisted to IndexedDB. No backend proxy. Each user runs their own OAuth client ID — that's why the README has fork-and-configure instructions.

3 · IndexedDB schema with 4 memory kinds.
daily · project · person · note. Each item has id · title · content · ts · tags[] · people[] · deadlines[]. Auto-extraction at T0 uses regex (tags via #word, people via capitalised multi-word patterns, deadlines via month-day patterns). T2/T3 adds AI classification on top.

4 · BroadcastChannel mesh integration.
On boot, emits `tool:ready` on `fall-signal` so sibling estate tools know botler is live. Listens for cross-tool events. Same pattern as ExitEngine. Zero coupling — botler works standalone, augmented when adjacent tools are present.

5 · Service worker skip-list for the AI path.
The SW caches the shell aggressively but skips intercepting esm.run, googleapis.com, anthropic.com, openai.com, huggingface.co. The model fetch and the OAuth flow MUST go direct or they break. SW intercepting third-party CDN fetches is the silent killer of in-browser AI tools.

The whole file: github.com/sjgant80-hub/botler

The hardest part wasn't the AI. It was the OAuth PKCE flow without a backend, and the SW skip-list logic.

If you build sovereign agents: those two patterns matter more than the model choice.
