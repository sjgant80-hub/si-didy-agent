POST 6 · AUDIT CHAIN · day 12
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
audience: LAYER 2 · security engineers · audit-curious devs · CISOs
hook: technical insight about Article 12 interpretation
carousel: optional · single shot of verifyChain() output + Git-like diagram

▼ COPY ▼
Article 12 of the EU AI Act says: high-risk AI systems must keep "automatic logs" over their lifetime.

Most teams interpret this as: write to a database. Done.

That's a fragile read.

What the regulators will actually want to verify: "show me your logs are tamper-evident."

The cleanest implementation: cryptographic prevHash chain.

Entry N includes:  ts · event_type · payload · prev_hash
Entry N's hash =   SHA-256(JSON.stringify(Entry N))
Entry N+1's prev_hash = Entry N's hash

Now any modification to any past entry breaks every subsequent hash. A regulator can clone your log, run a 5-line verification function, and trust the result.

This is how Git works. How Bitcoin works. How basically all auditable systems work.

Article 12 doesn't mandate this. But once one regulator asks for it, it becomes the standard. And the teams who didn't build it have to migrate every past log.

I baked this into fall-euaiact from day 1. The audit shim is 60 lines. Zero dependencies. Works in browser, Node, Cloudflare Workers, Deno.

SDK: github.com/sjgant80-hub/fall-euaiact/blob/main/sdk/audit.js

Audit chains are mostly a security concept right now. They're about to become a compliance concept.

Be ready before the standards body catches up.
